Privacy Policy

PLACEHOLDER FOR LEGAL REVIEW — this draft describes actual app behavior accurately but has not been reviewed by counsel. Review before store submission.

What we collect

  • Account: your email address and a password hash, for sign-in.
  • Shooting data you enter: rifle/barrel/load profiles, range sessions, shot coordinates, group statistics, chronograph velocity data, cleaning and scope logs.
  • Target photos: images you capture or upload for sessions are stored privately in your account's cloud storage.
  • Location (optional): if you grant permission, your GPS position is used to fetch local weather/elevation from Open-Meteo. Coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo for that request and are not stored on our servers.
  • AI assistant content: when you use "Ask yorT" or the ammo-box scanner, your question, selected shooting data, and any photos you attach are sent to Anthropic (Claude) via our server to generate the answer. Token counts are logged for cost tracking.

Where it lives

Your data is stored in a Supabase (PostgreSQL + Storage) project, scoped to your account with row-level security. Photos live in a private storage bucket under your user ID.

What we don't do

  • No selling of personal data.
  • No advertising or third-party analytics trackers.
  • No sharing of your data except the processors named above (Supabase hosting, Anthropic for AI answers, Open-Meteo for weather).

Your controls

  • AI consent: the AI assistant only runs after you accept its disclosure; declining disables it.
  • Deletion: "Delete Account" in the app permanently removes your account, all shooting data, and all photos. This cannot be undone.
  • Export: "Export my data" in Data & Records builds CSV files of everything on your own device, any time — including annotated images and certificates. Your data is yours.
  • Retention: if a paid plan ever lapses, your data is retained and stays viewable and exportable — nothing is deleted except by your explicit request.

Community data

Anonymized, aggregated data (never your name, email, serial numbers, or photos) may be published in annual community reports — for example, "rifles with a 1:8 twist shot best with…". Individual shooters are never identifiable.
